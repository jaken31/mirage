# Decode each frame in rounds, not residuals

**Recommendation: retrain the dynamics model as a MaskGIT-style predictor and decode each frame in a few confidence-ordered rounds (sweep 1, 2, 4 and 8 rounds), sampling within each round. Keep raster autoregressive decoding as the fallback and the quality ceiling. Do not lead with a residual or change-based predictor.** The project's freeze matches a failure the literature has named and measured: a model that predicts every output token independently learns only per-position marginals, so when the target has several valid joint outcomes, per-position argmax mixes them or falls back to the safest token ([Gu et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.02281)). Every published fix lets later tokens condition on tokens already chosen. That is the one thing the project's four failed remedies (temperature sampling, context corruption, scheduled sampling) and its decode-time coordination test (r76) did not provide. Iterative rounds recover most of the gap to full autoregression: in text, 4 rounds close about 82% of it and 10 rounds about 93% ([Ghazvininejad et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09324)). Genie already runs a token world model this way ([Bruce et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15391)). A residual parameterization points the wrong way: the literature's main failure mode for delta and copy models is copying too much, which is the problem the project already has. One caveat governs the whole plan. No paper measures "frozen frame share versus decoding scheme" on one model, so the claim that MaskGIT rounds will fix this freeze is an inference from neighbouring evidence, not a published result. The first experiment is designed to test it on two seeds before anything else is built.

Throughout, "the paper states" marks a claim read from the cited source. "Inference" marks reasoning that applies those results to this project and has not been measured here.

## The freeze is the textbook failure of independent per-cell prediction

The paper states the mechanism plainly. Gu et al. define a naive non-autoregressive translator as a product of per-position distributions, each conditioned only on the input. "Thank you" has several valid German translations, and such a model emits invalid mixes like "Danke Dank" ([Gu et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.02281)). Huang et al. show that maximum-likelihood training of such a model fits the marginals and **"drops all dependencies between tokens"** ([Huang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05975)). The visible symptom is falling back to safe tokens: Mask-Predict's one-pass output repeats tokens **16.72%** of the time on WMT14 En-De, falling to **0.72%** after 5 refinement rounds, and the authors tie that drop to the rise in quality ([Ghazvininejad et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09324)). Token-Critic names the same weakness in MaskGIT itself: accepting or rejecting each token independently "impedes capturing rich correlations between tokens" ([Lezama et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.04439)).

Inference: the robot arm fits this almost exactly. The arm moves a fraction of a cell per 2 ms step, so a cell on a moving link flips only when accumulated motion crosses a quantization boundary. The project's own freeze probe (r75) found moving cells keep their token 51% of the time in the truth. So each such cell faces a near coin-flip on *when* it changes, while the flips across a link are strongly correlated: they all change together or not at all. Per-cell argmax resolves each coin flip separately, and "keep" is a single token while "change" spreads its mass across several codes. Keep therefore wins the argmax even when the summed probability of change is above one half. That is "Danke Dank" in two dimensions, and it produces the 91-95% frozen share. Rollouts then compound it: a frozen frame becomes context, which makes "keep" likelier still, giving the 96-98% frozen-after-frozen rate against the truth's 65%.

The project's r76 test is the strongest evidence for this reading. Forcing cells to change together on frames the model expects to move brought the frozen share down to the truth's (16-24%). Coordination was the missing ingredient for *whether* the arm moves. But token accuracy stayed near an arm held still, and the thin forearm link tracked worse than held still for about 100 steps. So r76 also shows a second problem: the model's per-cell guess about *what a moving cell becomes* is weak. Inference: that is what a one-pass model should produce. It was never trained to predict a cell given its neighbours' outcomes, so it cannot place a link consistently. A decoder that conditions each cell on cells already committed attacks both problems. A decode-time patch on an unchanged model attacks only the first.

## Sampling alone cannot fix it, because published fixes pair sampling with a coordinated decoder

GAIA-1 is the closest published match to the symptom. The paper states that argmax decoding "generates futures that get stuck in a repetitive loop", with frames that "repeat themselves", and that **top-k = 50 sampling** fixes it ([Hu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17080)). Genie samples too, at **temperature 2** over 25 MaskGIT rounds ([Bruce et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15391)). Read in isolation, these suggest "sample instead of argmax".

**The project has already shown that sampling alone is not enough here.** In r64 and again in r72 (two seeds, two checkpoints each), raising the temperature took the frozen share from 90-95% down to 12-18% at T = 1.0. But frames changed only 4.2-5.4% of their cells against the truth's 13.3%, a link vanished from a whole 200-step window, and token accuracy never beat the held-still arm (23.8-24.8% against 24.1%). Sampling unfroze the frame counter without moving the arm. Context corruption (r73) and scheduled sampling (r74) also left rollouts frozen.

Inference: this does not contradict GAIA-1 or Genie, because neither samples cells independently in one pass. GAIA-1 decodes a frame token by token, each conditioned on the earlier tokens of that frame. Genie samples within MaskGIT rounds, each conditioned on the cells committed in earlier rounds. In both, once a few cells commit to "the forearm moved", later cells see that and follow. Independent sampling has no such channel: each cell flips its own coin, which produces scattered noise rather than a displaced link. That matches what r64 and r72 measured. The notes found **no system that decodes 16 or more discrete tokens per frame in one pass with independent per-cell argmax**. The only one-pass systems (STORM, one 32x32 categorical vector per frame) report trouble with small moving objects, scoring 16 on Breakout against IRIS's 84 ([Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09615)). So the published recipe is sampling inside a coordinated decoder. Sampling should return only as part of that decoder.

## MaskGIT rounds are the best-supported first move; raster AR is the ceiling

Both sequential options give the coordination r76 lacked. They differ in cost, in how much they change the model, and in how strong the evidence is.

**MaskGIT-style rounds (recommended first).** The paper states the recipe. Training samples a mask ratio per example, replaces those tokens with a mask token, and takes the loss on the masked positions. Decoding starts fully masked, predicts every masked cell in parallel, keeps the most confident, and re-masks the rest on a schedule ([Chang et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.04200)). A **cosine schedule wins** (FID 6.06 at 10 rounds, against 7.51 for linear at its best of 16). **More rounds are not always better**: each schedule peaks and then degrades, with cosine peaking around 8-12 rounds on 256-token images ([Chang et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.04200)). For world models, Genie uses 25 rounds per frame and masks training inputs at a Bernoulli rate drawn uniformly from 0.5 to 1 ([Bruce et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15391)). Copilot4D uses 10 rounds and adds three changes that suit this project. It uniformly noises up to 20% of the *unmasked* tokens, takes the loss on all tokens, and lets the sampler revise tokens already decoded. That cut its error from 0.82 to 0.58 Chamfer at the same step count ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01017v4)). MaskViT trains with a variable mask ratio specifically to close the train/inference gap, and was fast enough for real-robot planning ([Gupta et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11894v2)).

Inference: four things favour MaskGIT here. First, 64 tokens is small: text at 20-30 tokens recovers most of the autoregressive gap by 4 rounds ([Ghazvininejad et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09324)), so 4-8 rounds is the likely sweet spot, and the sweep must include 1 and 16 because too many rounds can hurt. Second, a model trained across mask ratios up to 1.0 contains the current one-pass predictor as its one-round case. So one checkpoint gives a like-for-like comparison against today's behaviour and a measured gain per round, which softens the cost of an irreversible layout change. Third, confidence ordering commits the high-confidence cells first, such as the thick base link that r76 already tracked for about 200 steps. The thin forearm, whose moved tokens are rare and spread across many codes, is then predicted conditioned on them. Fourth, Copilot4D's revisable sampling targets the specific risk of an early, wrong "keep" that later rounds cannot undo.

**Raster autoregressive decoding (fallback and ceiling).** IRIS is the closest published analogue in scale: 64x64 frames, 16 tokens per frame, a 10-layer, 256-wide transformer, raster order, and sampled tokens ([Micheli et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.00588v2)). Full autoregression is the quality reference in the text results. Mask-Predict at 10 rounds still trails it by 0.7 BLEU ([Ghazvininejad et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09324)). Its costs are concrete. It needs a causal mask inside the frame, inputs shifted by one token, and 64 sequential forward steps per frame even with a KV cache. That cost is why Delta-IRIS cut to 4 tokens per frame and REM moved to parallel prediction ([Micheli et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19320); [Cohen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05643)). On 256-token images, raster order is a poor fit: MaskGIT at 8 rounds scored FID 6.18 against 15.78 for a 256-step autoregressive VQGAN ([Chang et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.04200)). Inference: part of that gap is specific to large images, so on 64 cells raster AR may be competitive, but the evidence does not favour it. The project's architecture already requires an explicit attention mask in the forward pass and a fixed-size KV cache, so both options fit the existing model interface. Neither needs a new model class.

## Residual predictors make copying the default, which is the wrong direction here

The literature on delta and copy-based predictors is consistent, and it warns against this direction. A critical review of PredNet reports that it **falls back to copying the last frame** on sequences with many still frames ([PredNet review, 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.11902)). On Human3.6M, copying the last frame "significantly outperforms" every model tested ([Villegas et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01655)). DIAMOND gives the mechanism in its own setting. A noise-prediction objective makes the model "learn the identity function when the noise is dominant", and switching to predicting the clean target fixed compounding error at 3 sampling steps ([Alonso et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12399v2)). Regressing a delta with MSE has the same trap: the optimum is the conditional mean, and the mean of "moved left" and "moved right" is roughly no motion ([Mathieu et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05440); [SAVP, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01523)).

Inference: a residual head makes "hold still" the zero-effort output. That helps a model that cannot hold still and hurts a model that already freezes. A discrete change gate on the 8x8 grid ("did this cell change? if so, which code?") is more tempting, because it splits off the "keep" mass that argmax currently favours. But no world-model paper tests a per-cell gate over a discrete grid. If the gate is still predicted per cell in one pass, it keeps the independence problem and will still produce partial arms. Its loss would also be dominated by static cells unless rebalanced toward the 13% that change. It is worth trying only as an output head on top of the MaskGIT model, not instead of it.

The strong change-based result is Delta-IRIS. Its tokenizer's encoder and decoder are conditioned on past frames and actions, so **4 delta tokens per frame** beat IRIS at 16 and 64 tokens and trained about 10x faster ([Micheli et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19320)). Two details limit how far it transfers. Delta tokens alone were weak: removing the continuous state summary dropped the Crafter score from **42.47 to 25.92**. And it changes the tokenizer, which the project's whole evaluation is built on. ITC is the cheap end of this family: a decode-time choice, solved as optimal transport, between copying a previous-frame token and generating a new one, with no change to training ([Kim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16457)). Inference: r76 already showed the ceiling of decode-time fixes on this model. Coordination unfreezes the arm, but accuracy is bounded by weak per-cell predictions, and ITC does not change those. Continuous-output diffusion models (DIAMOND runs 64x64 at roughly 4-13M parameters, using about 12 GB of VRAM on an RTX 4090) are the documented cure for blur and drift at this scale ([Alonso et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12399v2)). But they would replace the tokenizer-based evaluation, and no 8 GB run has been published.

## Experiment order

Each stage gates the next. The gate is the project's existing rollout gate on 27 val episodes of 585 steps at context 15, and every training stage runs on two seeds, as r70-r74 did. "Pass" means three things. The frozen share is near the truth's 20.1%, with frozen-after-frozen near 65% and about 13% of cells changing. Token accuracy clearly beats the held-still arm (24.1%). And per-link pose error beats held-still for both links, the forearm especially.

Two inferences shape the details. First, Genie-style masking of every frame in the window keeps the loss on all positions, but it trains on partly masked context that inference never shows. That is why step 2's clean-context share is worth testing rather than assuming. Second, decode cost differs: steps 1-2 cost 1-16 full passes per frame, while step 3 costs 64 cached passes. Which is faster at about 10M parameters depends on kernel-launch overhead, so measure it rather than assume it (MAGVIT reports masked decoding 60x faster than autoregression at its scale: [Yu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.05199)). Delta-IRIS-style conditional tokenization and continuous diffusion outputs stay off the list unless the evaluation is rebuilt, since both remove the tokens that evaluation is built on.

## Conclusion

The project's experiments have already separated two things the literature usually bundles. Coordination decides *whether* a frame moves (r76 fixed the frozen share). Per-cell conditioning decides *where* it moves (r76 left accuracy at held-still levels). Sampling, corruption and scheduled sampling addressed neither, because none of them lets one cell see another's choice. MaskGIT-style rounds are the smallest change that supplies both. As a bonus, their one-round case reproduces today's model, so step 1 directly measures how much coordination is worth on this data. That makes the irreversible layout change a measured bet rather than a blind one.

The open risk is the one the literature cannot settle: whether enough of the forearm's error is mode-splitting, which rounds fix, rather than missing information in the context, which no decoder fixes. Step 0 and step 1's one-round versus multi-round comparison answer that directly. If multi-round decoding unfreezes the arm but the forearm still trails held-still, the bottleneck is the representation (thin links in 8x8 cells), and the conversation moves to the tokenizer, not the decoder.
